Nutrition

Eat for the work.
Carbs included

Most people I meet eat too little protein and are scared of rice. Both are fixable. I log my own food every day, and I teach clients to do the same until they no longer need to.

Carbs

Carbs are your best friend. They are the fuel your muscles burn when the set gets hard.

  • Stored carbohydrate powers heavy lifting, hard runs and long rides. Cut it and your sessions go flat.
  • Rice, roti, poha, oats, potatoes and fruit all count. None of them is the problem.
  • What usually needs trimming is the oil, the sugar and the portion, not the staple.
  • Put most of your carbs around training and the work feels easier.
Protein

You need less protein than the internet says, and probably more than you eat now.

  • The official minimum, about 0.8 g per kg a day, prevents deficiency. It is not a muscle-building target.
  • If you lift, 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg covers it. Beyond that there is no extra muscle.
  • Dal, paneer, curd, eggs, chicken, soya and whey all work. Whey is a milk product, not a drug.
  • Three or four meals with protein in each is easier than one giant shake.

    Myths

    Things I'll talk you out of

    General guidance for healthy adults. If you need a medical diet, that's a job for a registered dietitian, and I'll say so.

    Carbs make you fat+

    Body fat follows how much you eat over weeks, not one nutrient. Carbs are what hard training runs on, so we plan them around your sessions instead of cutting them.

    More protein is always better+

    Protein matters for muscle, but the returns flatten. Most people who lift do well at roughly 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight a day. Past that you are mostly paying for expensive calories.

    Protein powder ruins your kidneys+

    In healthy adults, protein at these intakes has not been shown to harm the kidneys. Whey is dried milk protein, a food, not a steroid. If you already have kidney disease, your doctor sets your protein, not me.

    You can only absorb 30 g at a time+

    Your gut absorbs nearly all the protein you eat, whatever the meal size. Spreading it over three or four meals is convenient and helps you hit the total. It is not a hard limit.

    Lifting will make me bulky+

    Muscle is slow to build and takes years of deliberate eating and training. What lifting gives most people first is strength, better posture and joints that complain less.

    No pain, no gain+

    Effort, yes. Pain, no. Sharp or joint pain is information, and we respond by changing the lift, the load or the range, not by pushing through it.
